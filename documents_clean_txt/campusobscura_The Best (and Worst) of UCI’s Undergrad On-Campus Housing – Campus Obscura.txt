Perhaps one of the most agonizing and treacherous decisions
a student will make during their college career surrounds
housing. How livable one’s dorm is can determine everything-
studies show that the quality of a residence hall significantly
impacts a student’s mental wellbeing and subsequently,
grade point average. Whether they know it or not, the choice
of on-campus housing can shape a student’s future.
Here at UCI, undergraduates have five options to choose
from. In order of the date they were constructed from oldest
to newest, they are: Mesa Court Classics, Middle Earth
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 1/15
Classics, Mesa Court Towers, Middle Earth Towers, and the
ACC Apartments.
I don’t live in student housing, and never have. I live with my
parents off-campus. This, of course, begs the question: in
what way am I qualified to settle the debate that has persisted
on the UCI subreddit and lunchtime discussions at UTC- the
debate on which on-campus housing community is the best?
My experience (or lack thereof) with student housing belies
the fact that I am in fact the perfect candidate to put an end to
this discussion for once and for all. Untainted by bias, and
armed purely with my own observations. conversations with
student residents, and my invariably correct opinions, this is
my comprehensive ranking that will surely answer the age-
old question: Which undergraduate housing community here
at UCI reigns supreme? And, as we’re about to see, why is it
not Mesa Court Classics?
Which brings us nicely to…
#5- Mesa Court Classics
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 2/15
It’s immediately apparent that the Mesa Court Classics
buildings lag far behind the other on-campus options.
Everything about these buildings, from the wooden and
stucco siding on the exterior to the furniture of the ground
floor common areas, is notably dated. It should come as no
surprise, then, that the Mesa Court Classics community dates
back to UCI’s founding- 1965.
Amirdeep, a second-year from India, has lived in both a
single-occupancy room in a Mesa Court Classics hall and a
quadruple-occupancy room in the Towers. He was more than
happy to give up his spacious Classics room, even if it meant
sharing a room with three other people.
“It’s not nice in there, man. There’s supposed to be a cleaning
staff but they don’t clean, and the whole floor shares a
bathroom.” Amirdeep said. For his hall, that was seven people
to one bathroom. “I was honestly so glad to get out of there.”
Amirdeep’s friend, Jerry, isn’t as lucky, The rooms
themselves, while livable, also aren’t quite as modernized as
their newer counterparts. As I enter Jerry’s room, my eyes are
immediately assaulted by the large fluorescent light panels
that belong in a classroom rather than a dorm room, fixed to
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 3/15
the popcorn ceiling. They cast a glossy, radiant glow on the
worn carpet, as well as the radiator with chipped paint that
looks like it hasn’t been touched since the building was
constructed.
The bathroom is even worse. Drab gray tile flooring, which is
mildly damp when I enter, along with shower walls covered
in a film of mildew, has me retreating as soon as I take a
cursory glance at the facilities. It would barely be passable
were it a public restroom.
So, what do the Mesa Court Classics halls have going for
them?
“Community,” Amirdeep responds. “The newer halls, like the
towers, aren’t so great for mingling. With the classics, people
would befriend their neighbors, [and] hang out in the
common areas. You don’t really get that at the towers.”
Perhaps the real dorm is the friends we made along the way.
#4- Middle Earth Classics
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 4/15
The Middle Earth Classics were built a decade after their
cross-campus counterparts, and it seems the designers
learned from their previous mistakes. The sienna stucco has
weathered the years much better, and these walls also
contain something that their predecessors lacked-
personality.
Instead of residence halls with uninspiring names like ‘Isla’,
‘Arroyo’, ‘Ciela’, words which would much rather belong on a
vocab sheet in an intro to Spanish class, Middle Earth’s
residence halls sport names like ‘Shadowfax’, ‘Oakenshield’,
and ‘Misty Mountain’. That alone might be enough to get any
prospective UCI applicants excited about the campus culture,
given they’re big enough of a nerd.
And when it comes to the facilities themselves, there’s much
less murmurs of discontent among the ranks of the residents
at the Middle Earth Classics, too. Despite the dated furniture
on par with those at Mesa Court, the bathrooms seem well
taken care of, and rooms seem updated with newer carpets,
as well as wider windows that let in a great amount of natural
light.
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 5/15
Spiro Sun is a third-year who’s called Middle Earth Classics
home for the past year. Like many of its residents, he’s had a
middling living experience.
“To be honest, it’s a lot less modernized than I expected,” Sun
says, having got a brief taste of some of the other halls on
offer at UCI. “I also visited the towers for SPOP (Student
Parent Orientation Program) and those rooms looked a lot
nicer. But I can’t complain because it could be a lot worse,
too.”
With single- and double-occupancy rooms, the Classics
represent the most expensive option on-campus. According to
Sun, those living in single-occupancy rooms can expect to pay
upwards of an eye-watering $5,500 per quarter.
Luckily, for those who aren’t keen on selling away their
firstborn son to the University of California system, things
get better.
#3- ACC Apartments
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 6/15
“Living in the apartments is basically just like living in any
other apartment building. The only difference is that all of
your neighbors go to school with you,” says Jessica Yang, a
third-year who was lucky enough to navigate through the
infamous housing application portal and come out the other
side with a spot in the Camino del Sol community, one of six
designed and managed by American Campus Communities-
the largest such company in the country.
Many residents are a fan of this alternative way of on-campus
living as opposed to dorms. Their concerns pale in
comparison to the ones fielded from the Mesa Court Classics
students. The rooms don’t quite match the pictures on the
UCI Housing website- but when does the product ever match
the advertisement? The rooms are dusty on move-in day, but
that’s nothing a few wet wipes can’t fix. And while the wifi is
prone to instability, students make do with cellular data
hotspots whenever outages occur.
Perhaps it was the effect that touring manky, grimy residence
halls in the Classics for three hours had on me, but the rooms
that I peek my head into at Camino del Sol seem like a
resplendent oasis of comfort. Hardwood flooring, sleek
furnishings included with the room, air conditioning
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 7/15
(something even the new towers can’t boast) and bathrooms
that don’t look like they could be found in Dante’s description
of the nine circles of hell mark a welcome change in quality.
Amenities such as a fitness center, outdoor kitchen area and
even a pool complete with cabanas truly do make me feel, as
the community boasts on its website, that I’ve ‘never lived
like this’.
But according to research from Dr. Joshua Brown, who
conducted a study with the University of Virginia concerning
dorm design and its impact on students, there may be an
unintended consequence to the way these apartments have
been constructed.
“There is a social cost associated with apartment-style
residence halls as opposed to the traditional layout.” Dr.
Brown says. “The apartment-style focus on privacy renders
them more isolating to students and lessens opportunities for
social interaction.” It is this ‘social cost’, he explains, that
leads to higher rates of loneliness that have measurable
impacts on academic performance- around a tenth of a grade
point according to his data.
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 8/15
This theory is further supported by conversations with
Camino del Sol’s undergraduate residents. “I definitely don’t
talk with my roommates much. [I] just [go] into my room
immediately after I come home from class,” Yang explains. “I
don’t even know who my neighbors are, honestly.”
#2- Middle Earth Towers
The Towers, despite their modern appearance and recent
construction, luckily don’t suffer from the design flaws
outlined by Dr. Brown’s research. The five-level low-rise,
boasting a facade of sparkling glass, concrete and exposed
steel, lines up its dorm rooms side-by-side flanking a central
hallway. Each floor has its own laundry area and study
facilities, making it easier still for students to interact with
one another.
The rooms also look spacious and livable. Tall floor-to-ceiling
windows ensure that residents are at little risk of
experiencing shortages of Vitamin D, and every housing unit
has its own bathroom, kept fresh and clean while having
enough space for the four to eight students that will share it.
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 9/15
The designers also haven’t neglected the Lord of the Rings-
themed touches that graced the Classic residence halls.
Inscriptions of Tolkein’s Elvish language have been carved
into the towers’ concrete base, while high-tech learning
spaces bearing names such as ‘Gandalf’s Classroom’ join
other amenities such as a gym and dining hall, Brandywine.
Now that we’ve narrowed down the winner to either one of
the Towers, what separates Middle Earth and Mesa Court in
this narrow two-horse race?
#1- Mesa Court Towers
My awarding the top spot to the Mesa Court Towers is
predicated on two points- dining and social life.
As every student who resides in the residence halls is
contractually obliged to obtain a meal plan, the food quality
at the dining halls is a crucial factor in my decision. To see for
myself which hall is better I dropped a combined $34 on
guest entry fees for Middle Earth’s Brandywine and Mesa
Court’s Anteatery, and I’m still angry about it.
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 10/15
The food on offer can only be described as typical buffet fare,
except worse. I get the same lunch at both halls on different
days, and by a slim margin, the Anteatery has my vote. The
pepperoni pizza at Brandywine is warm but very dry, like I’ve
left it in the box overnight. The spaghetti is mushy and the
pasta clearly overcooked. The only thing I know how to do in
a kitchen is bug my mother about when dinner will be
finished, and even I can still boil pasta al dente, or at least
better than what was put on my plate. Anteatery, by
comparison, is passable. The fact that students are forking
over four figures per quarter for this, though, is enough to
make me feel physically ill.
But conversations with diners at the Anteatery, including
friend groups with students dorming at both Mesa Court and
Middle Earth Towers, does elucidate me about the decisive
point that wins top spot for the former- the social scene.
“The Middle Earth [Towers] social scene is dead,” third-year
Tej Thakur says. “I mean, it probably has to do with the fact
that it’s all introverted STEM students, but people just stick to
themselves most of the time.”
https://campusobscura.wordpress.com/2024/06/11/the-best-and-worst-of-ucis-undergrad-on-campus-housing/ 11/15
Tej’s friend and Mesa Court Towers resident Daniel is quick to
chime in. “The atmosphere at Mesa Court [towers] is
awesome. The RAs really make an effort to make sure people
get along, and the events are nice. People show up for them.”
The fact that Mesa Court Towers only offers quadruple-
occupancy rooms doesn’t seem to give any of its residents any
cause for complaint- any issues I do hear seem to be related to
bad roommates rather than the room itself. The rooms are
spacious enough to house four people, and the facilities well-
equipped to handle them. And as Dr. Brown explains,
socialization is important for success, something the Mesa
Court Towers can offer in abundance.
So, at the end of it all, it’s the Mesa Court Towers that rules
the roost. With clean rooms, useful amenities, sociable
neighbors and better food (just), the Mesa Court Towers are
hereby declared UCI’s best on-campus housing for
undergrads.
I’ll still take my comfy little room in my parents’ house over
all that any day, though.
